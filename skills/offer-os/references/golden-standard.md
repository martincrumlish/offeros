# Golden Standard

OfferOS should reproduce the depth of a hands-on senior Codex build, not the thin first draft that a generic prompt produces.

Use this standard when judging whether an output is good enough.

## What Workshop-Grade Means

The final system should feel like a real launch package:

- The offer has a sharp buyer, painful problem, clear promise, and concrete deliverable.
- The design is not a generic template. It fits the category, price point, and emotional tone.
- The logo and graphics share one visual system.
- The sales page has direct-response structure, strong hero hierarchy, VSL area, pricing, stack, objections, and repeated CTAs.
- The page is coded, responsive, and visually checked.
- The PDF product is useful enough to sell or deliver.
- Ads include market diagnosis, angles, hooks, copy, creative concepts, and honest image provenance. When generated imagery is requested, the ad images come from `imagegen`, not SVG/PIL/text-card substitutes.
- Emails use a launch arc and direct-response logic.
- The VSL deck is paced for narration with low text.
- The dashboard previews assets instead of dumping links.
- QA catches broken links, missing assets, mobile overflow, placeholders, and weak consistency.

## Minimum Depth Targets

These are not hard word counts, but they prevent shallow output:

- Offer architecture: complete market/buyer diagnosis, offer stack, objections, proof strategy, pricing logic.
- `design.md`: concrete production rules, not mood-board language.
- Sales copy: full long-form page copy, not a section outline.
- Sales page: complete coded page with real copy and assets.
- PDF product: multiple sections/modules with tools, worksheets, examples, and action steps.
- Ads: at least 10 full ads, 10 creative concepts, 30 hooks, and top 3 test recommendation.
- Emails: at least 5 full emails; deep mode should prefer 7 unless the offer clearly needs fewer.
- VSL: 20 to 30 slides with speaker notes or narration guidance.
- Dashboard: modal/iframe/image previews and direct asset actions.

## Shallow Output Signals

Revise when you see:

- copy that could apply to any business
- generic benefits without mechanism
- a PDF that reads like an article
- emails that all repeat the same pitch
- ads that describe the product instead of the buyer state
- design described as "modern, clean, professional"
- page sections that do not have conversion jobs
- VSL slides that summarize instead of persuade
- dashboard cards that only navigate away
- artifacts labeled as generated images when they are hand-coded SVGs, screenshots, or PIL text cards
- a QA report that passes technical checks while ignoring buyer value

## Calibration Rule

If the output would embarrass a workshop leader when shown as a demo, it is not done.
